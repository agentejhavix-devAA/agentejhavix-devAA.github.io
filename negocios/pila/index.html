The rule

Four dates on the battery clock — the passport is the one that blocks the market

Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 applies to all batteries placed on the EU market. The digital passport is mandatory from 18 February 2027: without it, a covered battery cannot be placed on the market or put into service.

FAQ

What battery teams are asking right now

When does the battery passport become mandatory?
From 18 February 2027, each EV battery, LMT battery above 2 kWh and industrial battery above 2 kWh placed on the EU market or put into service must have a battery passport linked by QR code (Art. 77 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1542). [1][2]
Which batteries need a passport?
Electric-vehicle batteries, light-means-of-transport batteries (e-bikes, e-scooters) above 2 kWh, and industrial batteries above 2 kWh. Portable batteries follow different rules — labelling and removability/replaceability from 2027 — but no passport. [1][2]
What data does the passport contain?
Manufacturer identity, model, capacity, chemistry and materials, carbon footprint, performance and durability, and state-of-health history — plus recycled-content percentages (cobalt, lithium, nickel, lead) from 18 August 2028. The QR code on the battery links to the record. [1][3]
What happens if my battery has no passport?
From 18 February 2027 a covered battery without a passport cannot be placed on the EU market or put into service. Market surveillance can block, restrict or withdraw non-compliant batteries, and penalties are set by each Member State. [1][4]
What are the other key dates?
Carbon footprint declaration for EV batteries since 18 February 2025 and performance class since 18 February 2026; passport + QR from 18 February 2027; recycled-content documentation from 18 August 2028. [1][3]

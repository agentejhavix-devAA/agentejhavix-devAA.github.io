FAQ

What sole traders and landlords ask

Do I really need to file four times a year?
Yes, if you're in scope. You send four quarterly updates (5 August, 5 November, 5 February, 5 May) plus a final declaration that replaces your Self Assessment return, due 31 January after the tax year. HMRC confirms this in the step-by-step guide. [1][4]
What counts as qualifying income?
Gross income from self-employment and property, before expenses, added together. If the total is over £50,000 for the 2025/26 tax year, you're in from 6 April 2026. HMRC's own checker walks through the detail. [2]
What happens if I miss a deadline?
HMRC uses a points system: one point per missed update or return, and a £200 penalty once you reach four points. For 2026-27, HMRC confirmed no penalty points for late quarterly updates — but points still apply to late final declarations and tax returns. Don't rely on it. [3][5]
Does HMRC provide the software?
No. HMRC does not provide software — you need an MTD-compatible product (FreeAgent, Xero, QuickBooks and others). The official guidance links to the compatibility list. [1]
I'm under £50,000. Do I need to do anything?
Not yet, but the threshold drops to £30,000 from 6 April 2027 and £20,000 from 6 April 2028. Setting up MTD-compatible software and digital records now makes the transition painless. [2]
Does Quartly connect to my accounting software?
No. You answer 3 questions and everything is calculated in your browser. No data is sent to any server and no connection to your accounts is needed.

FAQ

What employers are asking right now

Is the Employment Rights Act 2025 in force?
The Act received Royal Assent in December 2025 and is being delivered in phases. Already in force: SSP reform and day-one paternity/unpaid parental leave (April 2026). Employer-facing duties ahead: harassment prevention 30 October 2026, dismissal changes 1 January 2027. [1][2]
What must I do about harassment?
From 30 October 2026, employers must take all reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment of employees — including harassment by third parties such as clients and customers. Where a tribunal finds the duty was breached, compensation can be uplifted by up to 25%. [2][4][5]
What changes on dismissals?
From 1 January 2027, the unfair dismissal qualifying period drops from two years to six months, and the cap on compensation is removed. Fire-and-rehire protections also take effect in January 2027. [1][2]
What about zero-hours and low-hours workers?
Workers on zero-hours and low-hours contracts will gain the right to guaranteed hours based on a reference period of hours actually worked, plus rights to reasonable notice and short-notice payment. Regulations are expected in 2027, with timing to be confirmed after consultation. [1]
What changed with Statutory Sick Pay?
Since 6 April 2026, the Lower Earnings Limit and the three waiting days were removed from SSP — eligible employees are paid from day one of sickness. [1][3]
Any changes to employment tribunal claims?
From 1 October 2026, the time limit for bringing a claim to the Employment Tribunal increases from three to six months (for breach-of-contract claims in Scotland, 9 November 2026). [1]
